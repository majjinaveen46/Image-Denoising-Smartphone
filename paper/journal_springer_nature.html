NeuroClean: High-Fidelity Smartphone Image Denoising via Hybrid U-Net–DnCNN Dual-Stream Deep Learning and Overlapping Hann-Window Tiling

Abstract
Smartphone cameras operate under acute physical form-factor limitations: micro-apertures and sub-micron to micron pixel pitches (1.0 μm – 1.4 μm) restrict photon capture, yielding severe Poisson photon shot noise, amplifier read noise, and chromatic grain under low-ambient illumination. Existing neural restoration approaches either suffer from oversmoothing of high-frequency photographic textures (in deep multi-scale autoencoders like U-Net) or fail to preserve low-frequency luminance continuity across large receptive fields (in feed-forward residual networks like DnCNN). Furthermore, deploying deep models on arbitrary, high-resolution smartphone captures (12+ to 100+ megapixels) induces fatal memory exhaustion or catastrophic boundary grid artifacts under naive spatial tiling. In this paper, we present NeuroClean, a unified deep learning and production deployment framework for smartphone image restoration. NeuroClean introduces a dual-stream Hybrid U-Net–DnCNN neural architecture that synergizes: (i) a multi-scale contracting-expansive U-Net branch with long skip connections to capture scene context and uniform illumination gradients, and (ii) a non-pooling 10-layer residual DnCNN branch operating at full spatial resolution to isolate high-frequency sensor noise without spatial decimation. The feature representations from both streams are fused through a 1×1 cross-channel projection layer to estimate the underlying residual noise field R(y), yielding the restored image via direct residual subtraction x̂ = clip(y - R_hybrid(y), 0, 1). To process arbitrary image dimensions without memory bloat or edge stitching seams, we engineer a 2D Hann-window overlapping cosine tiling engine with reflect padding and weighted overlap-add reconstruction. Evaluated on paired real-world smartphone photographs (1404×2040 native resolution), our hybrid model achieves a mean Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 28.60+ dB and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) of 0.8920+, representing a +4.56 dB and +0.1915 improvement over the raw degraded baseline (24.04 dB, 0.7005 SSIM), outperforming standard DnCNN (27.42 dB, 0.8650) and 3-level U-Net (27.95 dB, 0.8710) with statistically significant gains under Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < 0.001). The complete system is deployed as an open-source, production-grade cloud application featuring an asynchronous FastAPI inference engine, a responsive glassmorphic frontend with interactive comparison sliders, real-time blind noise estimation (σ̂), and an interactive 3D WebGL photodiode sensor simulation.
Keywords: Smartphone image denoising · Computational photography · Deep residual learning · U-Net · DnCNN · Hann-window tiling · Photon shot noise · Multimedia systems.

1 Introduction

Computational photography has emerged as the definitive driver of image quality improvements in modern mobile consumer electronics. Over the past decade, smartphones have superseded dedicated digital single-lens reflex (DSLR) and mirrorless cameras for everyday photographic capture [1]. However, smartphone camera hardware remains constrained by severe physical limitations. While full-frame and APS-C camera sensors possess pixel pitches between 3.7 μm and 8.4 μm, mobile camera sensors must compress tens of millions of photosensitive diodes onto sensor dies smaller than 1/1.3 inches, yielding pixel pitches typically spanning 0.8 μm to 1.4 μm [2].

Under low-light ambient conditions, high shutter speeds, or high ISO settings, the reduced physical collection area of each photodiode severely restricts the number of incident photons [3]. The resulting captures are degraded by composite stochastic noise processes: (1) Photon shot noise, arising from the Poisson distribution of photon arrival times [4]; (2) Thermal and analog read noise, generated by charge-to-voltage conversion amplifiers during analog-to-digital conversion (ADC) [5]; and (3) Chromatic noise, resulting from spatial interpolation during Bayer color filter array (CFA) demosaicing [6].

Historically, image denoising relied upon handcrafted spatial and transform-domain filters, such as Bilateral Filtering [7], Non-Local Means (NLM) [8], and Block-Matching and 3D Filtering (BM3D) [9]. While mathematically elegant, these techniques assume additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with stationary variance across the entire image plane. In real smartphone image signal processors (ISPs), however, noise is signal-dependent, heteroscedastic, and spatially correlated across RGB color channels [10]. Consequently, classical filters frequently blur fine organic textures (e.g., foliage, facial hair, fabric weaves) while leaving low-frequency chroma splotches intact.

The advent of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) revolutionized image restoration. Zhang et al. introduced DnCNN [11], demonstrating that predicting the residual noise field rather than the clean image directly accelerates training convergence and boosts restoration fidelity. Concurrently, Ronneberger et al.'s U-Net [12] gained widespread adoption in image-to-image translation tasks due to its multi-scale contracting-expansive paths and long skip connections that preserve macro-structural context [13].

Despite these advances, three critical bottlenecks persist: (1) The Context vs. Grain Dilemma: DnCNN maintains native resolution and isolates fine pixel grain, but its receptive field is bounded by network depth (21×21 pixels for 10 layers), rendering it blind to wide-area luminance mottling. Conversely, U-Net captures broad context via pooling, but destroys fine textures and introduces spatial phase distortions upon upsampling. (2) The High-Resolution Boundary Artifact Barrier: Direct evaluation on 12+ megapixel images triggers Out-Of-Memory (OOM) GPU exceptions, whereas naive non-overlapping patch tiling introduces unacceptable orthogonal boundary seam lines. (3) The Prototype-to-Deployment Disconnect: Research frequently terminates with command-line notebooks on downsampled crops rather than responsive, privacy-preserving, production cloud web applications supporting arbitrary dimensions.

1.1 Research Contributions

1. Dual-Stream Hybrid Architecture: A novel Hybrid U-Net–DnCNN deep neural network combining multi-scale contextual feature extraction (Stream 1) with non-pooling high-frequency residual grain modeling (Stream 2) joined via a 1×1 cross-channel fusion projection layer.
2. Deep Residual Formulation: Mathematical formulation of the restoration objective as deep residual subtraction x̂ = clip(y - R_hybrid(y), 0, 1), demonstrating faster convergence and superior preservation of image structural fidelity.
3. 2D Hann-Window Overlapping Cosine Tiling Engine: An engineered patch-inference engine utilizing 2D separable Hann cosine windows with 50% overlap and reflection padding, establishing a mathematical partition of unity that guarantees zero boundary seams across arbitrary input resolutions.
4. Comprehensive Empirical Benchmarking: Rigorous quantitative evaluation across paired real-world smartphone test sets (1404×2040 native resolution), achieving 28.60+ dB PSNR and 0.8920+ SSIM, significantly outperforming comparative baselines with Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank significance (p < 0.001).
5. Full-Stack Production Deployment & 3D WebGL Simulation: An open-source, full-stack cloud application featuring an asynchronous FastAPI microservice, a responsive React/TypeScript frontend with glassmorphic UI/UX, Dark/Light theme switching, live metrics, and an interactive 3D Three.js photodiode sensor simulation.

2 Related Work and Literature Review

2.1 Smartphone Image Noise Physics: Smartphone sensors operate with pixel pitches between 1.0 μm and 1.4 μm [2], [14]. Under photon-starved captures, quantum fluctuations in photon arrival follow a Poisson distribution: P(k; λ) = (λ^k e^(-λ)) / k! [4]. Combined with thermal agitation and readout fluctuations modeled as zero-mean Gaussian noise N(0, σ_r^2), the composite noise field v is heteroscedastic: Var(v | x) = αx + σ_r^2, where α scales with analog gain (ISO) [10]. Subsequent ISP demosaicing and tone mapping spatially correlate this noise across neighboring pixels [15].

2.2 Classical Spatial and Transform Filters: Bilateral Filtering [7] and Non-Local Means (NLM) [8] rely on spatial and radiometric similarity. BM3D [9] established the transform-domain standard via collaborative Wiener filtering. However, BM3D exhibits O(N^2) complexity and causes watercolor-like texture smudging on real smartphone noise [16].

2.3 Deep Residual Networks: DnCNN [11] introduced residual noise estimation L(Θ) = (1/2N) Σ || R(y_i; Θ) - (y_i - x_i) ||_F^2, accelerating convergence. However, its receptive field is strictly bounded by depth (21×21 pixels for 10 layers), rendering it incapable of resolving macro-scale color mottling.

2.4 Multi-Scale Contextual U-Net: U-Net [12] captures expansive global receptive fields through contracting pooling layers and expansive decoder paths with skip connections [19], [20]. However, spatial downsampling degrades fine photographic grain.

3 Mathematical Formulation and Problem Definition

Let x ∈ R^{H×W×3} denote the latent noise-free RGB photograph. The degraded smartphone observation is modeled as: y = x + v, where v ∈ R^{H×W×3} represents the composite noise field comprising Poisson photon shot noise, amplifier read noise, and chromatic demosaicing artifacts.

y = x + v (1)

Deep Residual Learning: In direct reconstruction, F_direct(y; Θ) ≈ x. In deep residual learning, the network is trained to predict exclusively the residual noise field: R(y; Θ) ≈ v = y - x. The clean estimate is recovered via residual subtraction:

x̂ = clip( y - R(y; Θ), 0.0, 1.0 ) (2)

Evaluation Metrics: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) = 10 · log10( MAX_I^2 / MSE(x, x̂) ). Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) = [(2μ_x μ_x̂ + C1)(2σ_xx̂ + C2)] / [(μ_x^2 + μ_x̂^2 + C1)(σ_x^2 + σ_x̂^2 + C2)]. Blind noise variance is computed using the high-frequency Immerkaer Laplacian operator [27]:

σ̂ = sqrt(π / 2) · (1 / [6(H - 2)(W - 2)]) Σ |(y * K_lap)(i, j)| (3)

4 Proposed Methodology and System Architecture

4.1 Dual-Stream Network: Combines Stream 1 (Contextual U-Net: 2 contracting encoder blocks with 32 and 64 filters, 128-channel bottleneck, expansive decoder with skip connections, outputting Φ_unet ∈ R^{B×H×W×32}) and Stream 2 (High-Frequency DnCNN: 10 non-pooling conv layers with Batch Normalization and ReLU, outputting Φ_dncnn ∈ R^{B×H×W×32}). Cross-stream concatenation Φ_concat = [Φ_unet || Φ_dncnn] is fused via a 1×1 convolution with Batch Normalization: Φ_fused = ReLU(BN(Conv1x1(Φ_concat))). A final 3×3 conv projects features to the 3-channel noise field R_hybrid(y), yielding x̂ = clip(y - R_hybrid(y), 0, 1).

Algorithm 1: 2D Hann-Window Overlapping Cosine Tiling Inference
Require: Input image y ∈ R^{H×W×3}, tile size T = 256, stride S = 128 (50% overlap)
Ensure: Restored high-fidelity image x̂ ∈ R^{H×W×3}
1: Construct 1D Hann window: w[i] = 0.5 * (1 - cos(2π(i + 0.5) / T)) for i = 0 to T - 1
2: Compute 2D separable window: W_hann = w ⊗ w ∈ R^{T×T×1}
3: Compute reflection padding (pad_h, pad_w) for stride S
4: y_pad = reflect_pad(y, pad_h, pad_w)
5: Initialize buffers: Output_Acc = zeros(H_pad, W_pad, 3), Weight_Acc = zeros(H_pad, W_pad, 1)
6: Extract overlapping tiles {T_k} at step S with coordinates {(y_k, x_k)}
7: Run batched model prediction: {P_k} = Model.predict(batch({T_k}))
8: for each prediction P_k do
9:   Output_Acc[y_k:y_k+T, x_k:x_k+T] += P_k * W_hann
10:   Weight_Acc[y_k:y_k+T, x_k:x_k+T] += W_hann
11: end for
12: Denoised_Pad = Output_Acc / max(Weight_Acc, 1e-6)
13: x̂ = clip(Denoised_Pad[0:H, 0:W], 0.0, 1.0)
14: return x̂

Proposition 1 (Partition of Unity): Under 50% overlap (S = T/2), adjacent Hann windows satisfy w[n] + w[n + T/2] = 0.5(1 - cos(2πn/T)) + 0.5(1 - cos(2π(n + T/2)/T)) = 1.0, guaranteeing mathematically seamless spatial reconstruction with zero boundary discontinuities.

5 Experimental Setup and Dataset

The dataset comprises 100 paired clean and noisy smartphone image sets captured at native resolution (1404×2040 pixels, 24-bit RGB). Clean references were captured at low ISO (50–100) mounted on a rigid tripod with long exposure times (1.0–4.0 s). Noisy captures were taken under identical framing at high ISO (1600–6400) with fast shutter speeds (1/50–1/200 s).

Models were trained on 128×128 patches with 64-pixel stride using the Adam optimizer (initial learning rate η = 10^-3 with cosine decay schedule, batch size 32, MSE loss) for 50 epochs on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU and Intel Core i9-13900K CPU.

The proposed Hybrid U-Net–DnCNN achieves 28.60 dB PSNR (+4.56 dB over noisy baseline, +1.73 dB over 3-layer CNN, +1.18 dB over standard DnCNN, and +0.65 dB over standard U-Net). Structural fidelity reaches 0.8920 SSIM (+0.1915 over raw capture). Non-parametric paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance across all comparisons (p_Holm < 0.001, r > 0.85).

Ablation Analysis: Omitting the DnCNN stream drops SSIM by 0.0210 (texture blurring); omitting the U-Net stream reduces PSNR by 1.18 dB (chroma mottling). The 1×1 fusion projection improves PSNR by +0.42 dB over simple concatenation. Residual learning converges in 22 epochs compared to 46 epochs for direct reconstruction.

Tiling Validation: Un-tiled GPU inference crashes due to Out-Of-Memory exceptions (>22.4 GB). Naive non-overlapping tiling introduces visible boundary grid seams (Mean Boundary Gradient Discontinuity MBGD = 0.0842) and reduces PSNR to 27.81 dB. Hann-window cosine tiling reduces seams to an imperceptible MBGD = 0.0012, attaining 28.60 dB PSNR with a low 620 MB VRAM footprint.

7 Discussion and Limitations

Practitioner Takeaways: (1) Dual-stream architectures resolving both contextual luminance and high-frequency grain are essential for real sensor noise; (2) Hann-window cosine tiling should be standard practice in mobile camera ISPs to prevent boundary artifacts; (3) Asynchronous web microservices dramatically increase research reproducibility.

Limitations: The network is specifically trained on stochastic sensor noise; optical defocus blur, heavy motion blur, or severe lens smudging are not restored.

8 Conclusion

We presented NeuroClean, a deep learning framework and production web application for smartphone image denoising. Combining a multi-scale U-Net branch with a 10-layer residual DnCNN branch and a 1×1 fusion projection layer, NeuroClean resolves both macro-luminance splotches and micro-pixel grain. The 2D Hann-window overlapping cosine tiling engine eliminates boundary seams while supporting arbitrary dimensions. Evaluated on paired real-world smartphone photographs, NeuroClean achieves 28.60+ dB PSNR and 0.8920+ SSIM, significantly outperforming classical and deep learning baselines (p < 0.001).
